## Later the same day — the corpus, explored (I73)

### Goal

The owner asked for a proper EDA in the museum, read live from the databases, and not a
dashboard decided in advance: an exploration that goes back and forth, interprets, makes
hypotheses and confirms them with numbers and figures, and teaches how.

### Done

- ADR 0028: chapters with dated readings, each resting on a check recomputed with the
  figures; the page says when a check stops holding. Catalogue metadata over every pack, grid
  measures over train packs only, hidden works nowhere.
- `tm.access` (refactor): the museum's display decisions moved from the host script into the
  library, shared by the host and the exploration.
- `tm.eda`: five chapters (contents, peak, SAUCE, composition, revival), a seeded permutation
  test, a Kitagawa decomposition; `tm eda` prints the snapshot; the host serves `/api/eda` and
  recomputes when PostgreSQL's write counter moves (looked at every 30 s).
- `/corpus` room in the museum, `en` and `fr`, SVG charts drawn at their measured width, every
  figure with its table; two series colours validated for colour vision deficiency on both
  surfaces. Checked in screenshots, desktop and phone, both locales, dark and light.

### What the exploration found (exploratory)

- The peak is more packs (1997: 1,008) and bigger packs (1996: median 24 works); after it,
  both fall (2000: 244 packs, median 2.5).
- SAUCE was adopted by groups: packs of 1994 are all or nothing; the packager guess is refuted
  (7% of fully stamped packs have a single date); groups by name prefix agree 83% against 72%
  by chance (p < 0.001). ACiD 13/16, iCE 1/21.
- Most of the fall of shades is composition (−5.9 of −9.5 points), confirming works.md live.
- The revival after 2013: median height 50 rows against 25, iCE 9.6% against 0.3%.

### Decisions

- Readings are written once and dated; numbers are live. No model call on refresh.
- The first draft found the largest packs in 1990 (five packs): years with fewer than 50 packs
  are left out of medians, and the page tells that story.

### Problems

- An older museum host (01:29) holds port 8737; Windows let a second bind silently. The test
  host ran on 8738; the owner's was left running.
- PowerShell 5.1 `Set-Content` corrupted the page's UTF-8 once; repaired. Python `write_text`
  writes CRLF on Windows; normalized before committing.
- Git's hooks need `pnpm` in bash: a scratch shim over `corepack pnpm`.

### Field notes added

SAUCE adopted group by group (ACiD early, iCE late); SAUCE nearly vanished 2005–2012; the
revival drew taller and brighter; the shades did not go away, the text came in.
